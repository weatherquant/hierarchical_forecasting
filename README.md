# Case-Adjusted Forecaster Variation in Operational Aviation Forecast Verification
## hierarchical_forecasting

A fair comparison of operational forecasters is challenging because the forecasts they issue differ across weather conditions, location and forecast horizon.

We analyzed almost 1.8 million Terminal Aerodrome Forecast and METAR comparisons from 109 forecasters at ten major Australian aerodromes in 2025.

Hierarchical statistical models were used to separate differences associated with forecasters from those associated with the forecast element, aerodrome and weather situation.

Weather conditions explained far more variation in accuracy than persistent differences between forecasters.

Aerodrome-day conditions accounted for 9.30% of the modelled variation, forecaster-by-element differences for 2.55%, and the overall forecaster effect for only 0.02%.

In other words, forecasters were not consistently better or worse across the whole forecast, but they did differ more for particular elements and rare hazards such as fog, thunderstorms, low visibility and low cloud.

Longer lead times increased false alarms for every hazard.

Later forecast updates reduced both misses, and false alarms for fog but had mixed effects elsewhere, such as reducing low cloud misses and increasing false alarms, while reducing thunderstorm false alarms but increasing misses.

These findings show that forecast performance depends on the weather situation, the forecast task and whether the concern is a missed event or a false alarm.

Operational review should therefore focus on difficult cases, specific elements and hazards, and the trade-off between misses and false alarms rather than on raw accuracy or individual rankings.
